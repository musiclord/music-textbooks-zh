增六和弦

Brian Jarvis

重點整理

  • 增六和弦是一組半音屬前和弦，包含le與fi之間的增六度[latex](\downarrow\hat6-\uparrow\hat4)[/latex]。
  • 有義式（It+6）、法式（Fr+6）與德式（Ger+6）增六和弦。
  • 增六和弦沒有根音，並解決到原位屬和弦。
譯註：本章採取不依根音分類增六和弦的分析方式：以le與fi形成的特徵音程、其餘組成音及解決方式辨認。這是這裡「沒有根音」的分析語境。

Key Takeaways

  • Augmented sixth chords are a group of chromatic predominant chords containing the interval of an augmented sixth between le and fi [latex](\downarrow\hat6-\uparrow\hat4)[/latex].
  • There are Italian (It+6), French (Fr+6), and German (Ger+6) augmented sixth chords.
  • Augmented sixth chords have no root, and they resolve to a root-position dominant chord.

概要

增六和弦是一類半音屬前和聲，名稱來自其中一個特定音程：le與fi之間的增六度[latex](\downarrow\hat6–\uparrow\hat4)[/latex]。義式、法式與德式增六和弦，除了這個音程之外，還包含一或兩個其他音級，如下表及例1所示。增六和弦可用於大調與小調，但小調較常見。

增六類型音級唱名
義式[latex]\hat{1}[/latex]do
法式[latex]\mathrm{\hat1\ and\ \hat2}[/latex]do與re
德式[latex]\mathrm{\hat1\ and\ \downarrow\hat3\ (or\ \hat1\ and\ \uparrow\hat2)}[/latex]do與me
（或do與ri）

Brief Overview

Augmented sixth chords are a category of chromatic predominant harmonies whose name is derived from the inclusion of a very specific interval: the augmented sixth between le and fi [latex](\downarrow\hat6–\uparrow\hat4)[/latex]. Each augmented sixth chord (Italian, French, and German) contains one or two other scale degrees in addition to this interval, as summarized in the chart below and illustrated in Example 1. Augmented sixth chords can occur in both major and minor keys, but they’re more common in minor.

+6 Type Scale Degrees Solfege
Italian [latex]\hat{1}[/latex] do
French [latex]\mathrm{\hat1\ and\ \hat2}[/latex] do & re
German [latex]\mathrm{\hat1\ and\ \downarrow\hat3\ (or\ \hat1\ and\ \uparrow\hat2)}[/latex] do & me
(or do & ri)

使用情境

你已熟悉如何找出和弦根音，以便作羅馬數字分析；但增六和弦通常不按根音分類，而是先辨認其中le與fi之間的增六度[latex](\downarrow\hat6–\uparrow\hat4)[/latex]，再依其餘音分類，如上表所示。「義式」「法式」「德式」是生動的名稱，未必對應歷史起源；三者都以這個特徵音程為基礎。

增六和弦是運用半音化加強和聲張力的另一種方式。它們主要作為屬前和聲，也可有裝飾作用，參見〈共同音和弦〉；在終止位置，通常直接進到原位V。它們可以加強推向半終止或正格終止的力量；V可以帶有七音，也可以包含[latex]\mathrm{cad.^6_4}[/latex]的裝飾。例3將三種類型都放入簡單的正格終止進行中。通常可預期le [latex](\downarrow\hat6)[/latex]在低音，而fi [latex](\uparrow\hat4)[/latex]可以在任何其他聲部。Ger+6後面通常接[latex]\mathrm{cad.^6_4}[/latex]，以避免原本會在G–D與F♯–C♯之間形成的平行完全五度；其他兩種類型之後也可以接[latex]\mathrm{cad.^6_4}[/latex]。在四聲部織度中，Fr+6與Ger+6不需要重複音；It+6只有三個不同的音，因此通常重複主音，因為它不是傾向音。

Context

You’re now familiar with the process of finding a chord’s root for Roman numeral analysis, but augmented sixth chords are not typically categorized by root. Instead, they are identified as chords containing the augmented sixth between le and fi [latex](\downarrow\hat6–\uparrow\hat4)[/latex] and further categorized according to the other notes in the chord, as shown above. The names “Italian,” “French,” and “German” are more colorful than historical, but each chord is based on this characteristic interval.

Example 2 shows the specific scale degrees of the augmented sixth interval and their resolution. Notice that both notes in this interval are active notes: each one resolves to sol [latex](\hat5)[/latex] from a minor second away.

Musical example

Static score pending; the original resource contains a musical example.

Example 2. Standard voice leading of the augmented sixth interval.

Augmented sixth chords are another strategy for creating harmonic intensification with chromaticism. They are mostly used as a predominant harmony (though they can serve an embellishing function as well—see Common-Tone Chords), and they lead directly to root-position V at a cadence point. They may intensify the push toward half and authentic cadences, and the V chord may have a seventh and/or include a [latex]\mathrm{cad.^6_4}[/latex]. Example 3 shows all three types in a simple cadential setting (authentic cadence versions). Note that you can expect that le [latex](\downarrow\hat6)[/latex] will be the bass for this chord, but fi [latex](\uparrow\hat4)[/latex] can be in any other voice. The Ger+6 is typically followed by a [latex]\mathrm{cad.^6_4}[/latex], which serves to offset the parallel perfect fifths that would have happened between G–D and F♯–C♯; the [latex]\mathrm{cad.^6_4}[/latex] may follow the other types as well. In a four-voice texture, the Fr+6 and Ger+6 don’t require doubling; in the It+6, which has only three unique pitches, the tonic is typically doubled because it is not an active note.

Connection to the lament-bass progression

When augmented sixth chords precede a half cadence, they resemble a phrygian half cadence and/or the lament-bass progression, where the iv6 chord is substituted with an augmented sixth chord by replacing fa with fi [latex](\hat4[/latex] with [latex]\uparrow\hat4)[/latex]. 分析時如何辨認增六和弦

增六和弦不像先前熟悉的和弦那樣以根音為分類基礎，因此需要換一種辨認方式。如果試著把和弦按三度堆疊來判定性質，會因為其中出現減三度而感到困惑。最簡單的方法，是記住低音le–sol [latex](\downarrow\hat6-\hat5)[/latex]可以支持這種進行：當這兩個音級上方出現和弦，而且低音le [latex](\downarrow\hat6)[/latex]上方的和弦也包含fi [latex](\uparrow\hat4)[/latex]，就很可能找到了增六和弦。接著再看其餘組成音，判斷是義式、法式或德式。

Recognizing augmented sixth chords when analyzing

Because augmented sixth chords are not root-based like you're used to, you need another strategy to find them. Trying to determine the quality by stacking the chord in thirds would become confusing because it would contain a diminished third. The easiest method is simply to memorize that the bass motion le–sol [latex](\downarrow\hat6-\hat5)[/latex] can support this progression and if chords occur above those scale degrees and the chord with le [latex](\downarrow\hat6)[/latex] also contains fi [latex](\uparrow\hat4)[/latex], then you've likely identified an augmented sixth chord. From there, just determine the specific subtype (Italian, French, or German) by looking at the remaining chord members.

大調中的Ger+6：me與ri [latex](\downarrow\hat3[/latex]與[latex]\uparrow\hat2)[/latex]

Ger+6用於大調時，常將me [latex](\downarrow\hat3)[/latex]改以等音拼寫，避免同一音名字母連續搭配不同的變音記號。這是因為Ger+6通常解決到[latex]\mathrm{cad.^6_4}[/latex]，後者已包含mi [latex](\hat3)[/latex]。用ri [latex](\uparrow\hat2)[/latex]代替me [latex](\downarrow\hat3)[/latex]，也能更清楚呈現該聲部的上行方向。例5展示這種Ger+6的變體拼寫。

Ger+6 in major keys: me vs. ri [latex](\downarrow\hat3[/latex] vs. [latex]\uparrow\hat2)[/latex]

When a Ger+6 is used in major keys, me [latex](\downarrow\hat3)[/latex] is often respelled to avoid writing the same letter name twice in a row with different accidentals, since Ger+6 typically resolves to a [latex]\mathrm{cad.^6_4}[/latex], which already contains mi [latex](\hat3)[/latex]. Using ri [latex](\uparrow\hat2)[/latex] instead of me [latex](\downarrow\hat3)[/latex] also allows for a clearer indication of the ascending motion of the line. The German Diminished Third Chord

In the 19th century, composers introduced a variant of the Ger+6 that used fi [latex](\uparrow\hat4)[/latex] in the bass instead of le [latex](\downarrow\hat6)[/latex] (Example 6). This inverts the augmented sixth interval, making it a diminished third instead. The Gero3 is very similar to viio7/V—the only difference is that Gero3 has le [latex](\downarrow\hat6)[/latex]  but viio7/V has la [latex](\hat6)[/latex]—and they both resolve to root-position V.

Musical Example

Ernesto Nazareth's tango "Remando" (Example 7) uses a Ger+6 in m. 60 as part of the cadential progression. Notice the stepwise bass motion beginning in that measure, la–le–sol [latex](\hat6-\downarrow\hat6-\hat5)[/latex], as a technique to approach the augmented sixth chord by step in the bass. The melody of this dance features many accented passing tones, so the C♯ during the Ger+6 should be considered embellishing given that context. As is typical with the Ger+6, the following dominant chord is embellished with a [latex]\mathrm{cad.^6_4}[/latex].
