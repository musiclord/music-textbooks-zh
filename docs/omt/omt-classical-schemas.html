流行音樂中的古典圖式

Bryn Hughes and Kris Shaffer

重點整理

本章圖式都以共通寫作時期音樂常見的模式為基礎。

  • 哀嘆圖式是四和弦圖式，低音沿小調四音列，從主音下行至屬音：do–te–le–sol [latex](\hat1-\downarrow\hat7-\downarrow\hat6-\hat5)[/latex]。典型配法是i–♭VII–♭VI–V，但te [latex](\downarrow\hat7)[/latex]可以用許多不同和弦配和聲。
  • 五度循環圖式至少有四個和弦，各和弦根音依序下行完全五度。它有許多變化，也不一定從主和弦開始。
譯註：原文把典型小調哀嘆的主和弦寫成大寫I；本章例1實際譜面標Dmi與小寫i，因此繁中在這個小調典型中使用i。大I也可能出現在混合調式的變體，但不等同本例的小主和弦。

Key Takeaways

The schemas discussed in this chapter are all based on those often found in common-practice music.

  • The lament schema is a four-chord schema that descends down the minor tetrachord from the tonic to the dominant: do-te-le-sol [latex](\hat1-\downarrow\hat7-\downarrow\hat6-\hat5)[/latex]. Most typically, this is harmonized by I–♭VII–♭VI–V, however, te [latex](\downarrow\hat7)[/latex] can be harmonized in numerous ways.
  • The circle-of-fifths schema is at least four chords in length, and it consists of chords whose roots descend by perfect fifth. This schema has many possible variations and does not necessarily start on the tonic.

哀嘆

「哀嘆」進行得名於早期古典音樂：這個幾乎總在小調中的進行，常被用作哀嘆歌曲的固定低音。例如Henry Purcell歌劇《Dido and Aeneas》（《狄朵與埃涅阿斯》）中的〈狄朵的哀歌〉，以及J. S. Bach《B小調彌撒》中的〈被釘十字架〉。這些古典例子的詳情，請見〈固定低音〉章。

Dire Straits的〈Sultans of Swing〉，是流行／搖滾音樂中典型的哀嘆圖式例子（例1）。這個樂句的低音沿小調四音列do–te–le–sol [latex](\hat1-\downarrow\hat7-\downarrow\hat6-\hat5)[/latex]下行，以i–♭VII–♭VI–V三和弦配和聲。中間兩個和弦使用切分，而且時值較短，讓樂句末尾的屬和弦有空間再加入七音，形成銜接下一次和弦進行的回轉。

Lament

The “lament” progression is so named because in early classical music, this chord progression (almost always in minor) was used as the ground bass for songs of lament. Examples include “Dido’s Lament” by Henry Purcell, from the opera Dido and Aeneas, and J.S. Bach’s “Crucifixus,” from his Mass in B Minor. For more on those classical cases, see the Ground Bass chapter.

Dire Straits' "Sultans of Swing" provides a classic example of the lament in a pop/rock setting (Example 1). The phrase descends through the minor tetrachord do-te-le-sol [latex](\hat1-\downarrow\hat7-\downarrow\hat6-\hat5)[/latex] and is harmonized with diatonic triads: I–♭VII–♭VI–V. The middle two chords are syncopated and given less duration in order to make room for the seventh to be added to the dominant chord at the end of the phrase, which provides a turnaround to repeat the chord progression.

五度循環

譯註：原章錨點使用大寫Circle-of-fifths，其他章卻用小寫circle-of-fifths；本地補上小寫別名，兩種來源連結都保留。

這個圖式中，每個和弦的根音都下行五度，接到下一個根音。它常出現在小調，從i開始，結束於關係大調。和創作歌手進行一樣，它也有些調性模糊：起始和弦容易被當成主和弦，但VII到III又很容易被聽成關係大調的V–I。因此，這個進行也能用來從關係小調移向關係大調。

Aqua〈Barbie Girl〉的副歌，提供一個調性略為模糊的五度循環圖式例子（見例3）。

流行／搖滾音樂中，也有更長的五度循環圖式。

Radiohead〈You and Whose Army?〉（例5）的五度循環圖式稍微複雜一些。這裡使用帶半音變化的模進，每一對有五度關係的和弦，都在局部形成下一個和弦的副ii–V進行，很像爵士樂中的副ii–V進行。和〈Barbie Girl〉一樣，這首歌也有調性模糊：五度循環持續推動音樂，卻沒有強烈確立主音。E大調與C♯小調都能成立，因此下方分析同時列出兩種理解。

Circle-of-Fifths

In this schema, each chord’s root moves down by fifth to the next root. This progression often happens in minor, beginning on i and ending on the relative major. Like the “singer/songwriter” progression, there is some key ambiguity in this progression, as the starting chord is easily considered tonic, but the motion from VII to III can easily be heard as V–I in the relative major key. And indeed, it can be used to move from the relative minor to the relative major.

The chorus of Aqua's "Barbie Girl" provides a somewhat tonally ambiguous example of the circle-of-fifths schema (see Example 3).

Example 3. Aqua, "Barbie Girl" (1997).

The verse of Muse’s “Thoughts of a Dying Atheist” also includes a four-chord circle-of-fifths schema, immediately following the lament discussed above. This progression immediately repeats, returning to the initial minor key. However, the second time through, this lament–circle-of-fifths pattern leads to a chorus in the relative major (taking the III chord as the new tonic).

Longer examples of the circle-of-fifths schema can be found in pop/rock music, too.

Radiohead's "You and Whose Army?" (Example 5) presents a slightly more complex instance of a circle-of-fifths schema. Here we get a chromatic sequence of the schema, in which each pair of fifth-related chords functions locally as an applied ii–V progression of the following chord, much like the applied ii–V progressions found in jazz. Like "Barbie Girl," "You and Whose Army?" is tonally ambiguous. The circle-of-fifths schema propels the music forward, without ever strongly confirming the tonic. Both E major and C♯ minor are viable interpretations, and thus have both been provided in the analysis below.
