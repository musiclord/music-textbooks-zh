嘉蘭特圖式

Mark Gotham and Kris Shaffer

重點整理

  • 圖式是基本的音樂型態。十八世紀的作曲家與即興演奏者會學習並運用它們，作為創作新音樂的簡單基礎。
  • 這些型態種類很多，通常與音樂中的特定位置或功能有關，例如用於開頭。

圖式是「慣用的音樂樂句」（Gjerdingen 2007，6），提供旋律、和聲及節奏／拍節的骨架，供人創作嘉蘭特風格的新音樂。「圖式」可以有三種具體含義。第一，它是原型，也就是某種常見型態的理想化版本；第二，它可以是實例，也就是與原型相似的某個具體型態；第三，它可以是理論，用來解釋經常出現的音樂現象。理解圖式時，這三種觀念會一起運作：我們把某個具體型態，也就是實例，看成一般理想型態，也就是原型的一個版本；這種關係又幫助我們理解它在特定樂段中如何發揮作用，也就是理論層面。

圖式常有名稱。有些根據早期理論家的描述命名，例如 Joseph Riepel 描述過 Monte、Fonte 與 Ponte；有些則以理論家的姓名命名，例如 Meyer 圖式取名自 Leonard Meyer。

定義圖式時，既要看其中發生什麼，也要看它通常出現在作品的哪個位置。「發生什麼」涉及兩個以上的「階段」，大致就是相繼發生的音樂事件，包含：

  1. 旋律特徵：以音階級數表示，通常同時列出最高的旋律聲部與最低的低音聲部。
  2. 和聲特徵：以數字低音記號表示。
  3. 拍節特徵：標示各階段位於小節強拍（S）或弱拍（W）。

「出現位置」大致區分為通常用於樂曲開頭的起始圖式、用於延續的圖式，以及作為結尾的終止圖式。本章各介紹一種類型；〈嘉蘭特圖式摘要〉則列出更多例子。

Key Takeaways

  • Schemas are basic musical patterns that composers and improvisers of the 18th century would learn and use as a simple basis for creating new music.
  • There are many types of patterns, and they are typically associated with a particular place or function within the music, for instance as a way of beginning.

Schemas are “stock musical phrases” (Gjerdingen 2007, 6) that act as melodic, harmonic, and rhythmic/metric skeletons for creating new music in the Galant style. We can apply the term "schema" in three specific ways. First, a schema is a prototype—an idealized version of a common pattern. Second, a schema can be an exemplar—a single pattern that resembles the prototype. Third, a schema can be a theory—an explanation of a commonly occurring musical event. All of these ideas go into how we understand schemas. We understand an individual pattern (exemplar) as a version of an ideal general pattern (prototype), and that relationship helps us understand how that pattern is functioning within a particular passage of music (theory).

Schemas are often given names, sometimes based on descriptions from earlier theorists (the Monte, Fonte, and Ponte were described by Joseph Riepel, for example) or, at other times, named after theorists themselves (the Meyer is named after Leonard Meyer).

Schemas are defined both by what happens in the schema and by where it typically appears in a piece. The "what" part involves two or more "stages" (basically, events) and includes:

  1. melodic features, shown by scale degrees, usually for both the top (melodic) and bottom (bass) voices;
  2. harmonic features, shown with figured bass notation
  3. metric features, showing whether a stage occurs on a strong ("S") or weak ("W") beat of the bar

The "when" part basically distinguishes between schemas that are typically used to start a piece ("opening gambits"), as a "continuation," or as a concluding "cadence." This section introduces one of each type, and the Galant Schemas – Summary chapter sets out many more examples.

As the Galant Schemas – Summary chapter shows, there are many other schemas with a similar pattern to the Meyer, such as the Pastorella, the Jupiter, and the Aprile. In each of these cases, the schema starts with a tonic chord, moves away for two stages, and then returns to the tonic at the end. Like the Meyer, they are prototypes for the first phrase of an opening theme. As with the Meyer, the bass and harmonic structure are less fixed than the melody. The two central stages may articulate dominant harmony in all three schemas, and the second stage is also commonly accompanied by predominant harmony.

When each stage of a Meyer schema is given one measure of music, it is commonly found in the presentation or antecedent part of an opening theme. If those stages occur at the rate of two per measure, the Meyer may form a basic idea that would be followed by a closing gesture, such as the Prinner described below.

Closing Gestures Such as the Prinner

The Prinner is a typical response to an opening schema. It has four stages corresponding to four bass notes: fa – mi/me – re – do [latex](\hat4–\hat3–\hat2–\hat1)[/latex]. The Prinner’s melody typically accompanies the bass in parallel tenths: la/le – sol – fa – mi/me [latex](\hat6–\hat5–\hat4–\hat3)[/latex]. Harmonically, the fa ([latex]\hat4[/latex]) and do ([latex]\hat1[/latex]) bass notes tend to take [latex]^5_3[/latex] chords while the two middle bass notes, mi/me ([latex]\hat3[/latex]) and re ([latex]\hat2[/latex]), take [latex]^6_3[/latex] chords. There is often a 7–6 suspension on the third stage:

Stage 1 2 3 4
Meter S W S W
Melody la/le
[latex]\hat6[/latex]		 sol
[latex]\hat5[/latex]		 fa
[latex]\hat4[/latex]		 mi/me
[latex]\hat3[/latex]
Bass fa
[latex]\hat4[/latex]		 mi/me
[latex]\hat3[/latex]		 re
[latex]\hat2[/latex]		 do
[latex]\hat1[/latex]
Figures 5 6 7-6 I
Roman numerals IV I vii I

There are a few variants on the Prinner, including one that modulates to the dominant by using a version of the Prinner transposed up a fifth. In this version, the first stage of the Prinner is still a [latex]^5_3[/latex] chord and can also still be viewed as a subdominant (IV) chord, but now relative to the dominant key.

The Prinner can also be used to modulate from the tonic to the dominant. This variant is called the Modulating Prinner:

作業

學習圖式，確實需要親手練習。

  1. 演奏：先把提供檔案中的範例演奏一遍，最好在幾個不同調上練習。原文說明：MuseScore 可從 Notes／Transpose 功能表移調。
  2. 記憶：試著記住一些型態，可用以下方式自我測試：
    • 寫在紙上，先從空白音階開始。
    • 不看譜，憑記憶演奏。
  3. 裝飾：圖式幫助音樂形成結構，但本身還不算完整樂曲；從骨架到真正的音樂，需要補上許多細節。因此，試著在這些基本型態上即興加裝飾，從簡單的迴音、經過音等開始，再嘗試更大的改變。
  4. 完整樂曲：熟悉個別圖式後，試著依它們通常出現的位置與次序組合成曲。可以先使用以下範本：
    • 範本 1：PDF。
    • 範本 2：.mscz、.mxl、PDF。
譯註：Notes／Transpose 是原書提供的 MuseScore 功能表名稱；不同版本的介面名稱與位置可能不同。

每份範本都結合了幾種圖式，可以視為樂曲的原型；既示範圖式如何運作，也為風格仿作練習提供框架。使用這些範本時，要在骨架上加入豐富的音樂性格與裝飾。以下是幾個入門建議：

  • 節奏：選一個有特色的節奏，作為全曲基礎並經常使用，但不要每次完全相同，看看能變出多少種樣貌。
  • 旋律：加入裝飾，例如在某些級進中加入迴音，或填入大跳之間的音。
  • 伴奏：以一種以上有特色的型態配置和弦伴奏，例如阿爾貝提低音。
  • 織度：尤其在較長的作品中，改變同時出現的聲部數，以及它們之間的關係。

Assignments

Learning schemas really calls for hands-on practice.

  1. Playing: Begin by playing through these examples from the files provided, preferably in a range of different keys. (Note: you can transpose scores in MuseScore with the Notes menu: Notes/Transpose).
  2. Memorization: See if you can memorize some of these patterns. Test yourself by:
    • writing them out on paper (start with a blank scale)
    • playing them from memory
  3. Embellishment: Schemas help to structure music, but they are not really musical pieces in themselves: it takes a lot of fleshing out to get from these skeletons to real music. That being the case, try improvising embellishments of these basic patterns. Start with simple turns, passing notes and the like, then move on to more ambitious changes.
  4. Full pieces: When you're confident with individual cases, try piecing them together, according to their usual position and ordering. Start by using templates like these:
    • Template 1: .pdf
    • Template 2: .mscz, .mxl, .pdf

These templates each provide a combination of schemas which can be thought of as prototype pieces, both to illustrate how they work and as a template for scaffolding exercises in pastiche composition. Use these templates, but bury them beneath layers of musical character and embellishment. Here are some tips for getting started:

  • Rhythm: Try picking a single characteristic rhythm to serve as the basis for your piece and use it often (but not exactly: see how many different ways you can adapt it).
  • Melody: Introduce embellishments, decorating some stepwise motions with turns, for instance, and filling in some large leaps.
  • Accompaniment: Use one or more characteristic patterns for chordal accompaniments, like the Alberti Bass.
  • Texture: Particularly for longer pieces, vary the number of voices present and the way they relate.
