主歌—副歌曲式

Bryn Hughes and Megan Lavengood

重點整理

  • 主歌—副歌曲式是靈活多變的歌曲形式，1960年代迅速成為搖滾樂的主流，此後持續占有主導地位。
  • 在主歌—副歌曲式中，歌名歌詞、最容易記住的音樂與主要敘事，分配在兩個核心段落：主歌與副歌。

主歌—副歌曲式之所以如此命名，是因為最重要的兩個段落是主歌與副歌。其他可能出現的段落還有前副歌、橋段與後副歌。

Key Takeaways

  • Verse-chorus form is a versatile song form that rapidly took over rock-and-roll in the 1960s and has dominated the genre ever since.
  • In verse-chorus form, the title lyrics, the most memorable music, and the main narrative are split between two core sections: the verse and the chorus.

Verse-chorus form is so named because the two most important sections are the verse and the chorus. Other possible sections in verse-chorus form are prechorus, bridge, and postchorus.

A prototypical verse-chorus form song is illustrated in Example 1.

“Livin’ on a Prayer” by Bon Jovi follows a typical verse-chorus form (Example 2). It also illustrates common usage of five core sections in verse-chorus forms.

Example 2. “Livin’ on a Prayer” by Bon Jovi (1986).

Cycles

Notice that the sections used in “Livin’ on a Prayer” recur and seem to follow a pattern. Sections within a verse-chorus form have certain prototypical orderings and groupings. The verse, prechorus, chorus, and postchorus sections, for example, always progress in this order (though they don’t all need to be present). These groupings are referred to as cycles. In “Livin’ on a Prayer”:

  • After an extended intro, the first cycle begins:
    • A verse at 0:47
    • At 1:18, a prechorus increases energy and tension…
    • …into the chorus at 1:34.
  • After a brief interlude, this cycle is repeated beginning at 1:54.
  • A final cycle at 3:00 begins with a guitar solo (functioning as a bridge and replacing the verse in this cycle).
  • The cycle is followed by repetitions of the chorus, and a fadeout ends the song.

Sections within Verse-Chorus Form

Terms, concepts, definitions, and notational guidelines are taken from common convention and a combination of the resources listed below under Further Reading.

主歌（V）

  • 主歌屬於歌詞變動型，歌詞常用來推進敘事。
  • 1960年代以前，主歌通常和聲封閉。
  • 從1960年代開始，主歌越來越常呈現和聲開放（Summach 2012，114）。
  • 主歌和歌節一樣，通常以主和聲起始。
查看英文原文

Verse (V)

  • Verse sections are lyric-variant and often contain lyrics that advance the narrative.
  • Until the 1960s, verse sections tended to be harmonically closed.
  • Beginning in the 1960s, verse sections became more and more likely to be harmonically open (Summach 2012, 114).
  • Verses (like strophes) tend to begin on-tonic.

前副歌（P）

  • 辨識前副歌最容易的線索，是能量增加。
  • 前副歌具有許多srdc中d句的功能特徵：片段化、和聲節奏加快、離開主和聲，以及和聲開放。[1]
查看英文原文

Prechorus (P)

  • Prechorus sections can be recognized most easily by energy gain.
  • They bear many of the functional characteristics of the d phrase in srdc—fragmentation, acceleration of harmonic rhythm, movement away from tonic harmony, and harmonic openness.[1]

副歌（C）

  • 副歌屬於歌詞固定型，包含歌曲主要的歌詞材料，例如歌名或容易記住的歌詞鉤句。
  • 副歌功能的另一項典型特徵，是相較主歌更強烈的音樂表現，例如更濃密或活躍的器樂織體、突出的伴唱，或音域更高的旋律（Summach 2012，106）。
  • 副歌最常以主和聲起始，但並非只能如此。
  • 日常談話有時把副歌與疊句混用，但討論音樂理論時，要注意區分。副歌本身構成完整段落；疊句則包含在某個段落之內，詳見下文。
查看英文原文

Chorus (C)

  • Chorus sections are lyric-invariant and contain the primary lyrical material of the song (the title lyrics and/or lyrical hook).
  • Chorus function is also typified by heightened musical intensity relative to the verse, including features like “a more dense or active instrumental texture; prominent background vocals; and/or a higher register melody” (Summach 2012, 106).
  • Choruses most frequently (but not exclusively) begin on-tonic.
  • Although the terms chorus and refrain are often used interchangeably when speaking colloquially, take care to keep them separate when discussing music theory. Chorus sections are distinct from refrains because choruses constitute an entire section by themselves, whereas refrains are contained within a section (as described below).

後副歌（Z）

  • 後副歌是接在副歌之後的短段落，只負責結束這一輪循環，而不是引入或過渡到下一輪的開頭（Spicer 2011，第9段）。
  • Destiny’s Child的〈Independent Women, Pt. 1〉在1:18有明確的後副歌，以「女孩，我不知道妳能那樣跳」這句歌詞開始。
查看英文原文

Postchorus (Z)

  • A postchorus is a short section that follows a chorus and serves only to close the cycle (not to introduce or transition to the beginning of the next cycle) (Spicer 2011, para. 9).
  • A clear postchorus can be heard in “Independent Women, Pt. 1” by Destiny’s Child at 1:18, beginning with the lyric “Girl, I didn’t know you could get down like that.”

橋段（B）

橋段在主歌—副歌曲式中，是使用方式相當靈活的段落類型。

  • 主歌—副歌形式的歌曲，橋段通常只出現一次，後面接全曲最後的副歌，或最後的前副歌加副歌。
  • 在一輪循環內，橋段會取代主歌或前副歌，也可能兩者都取代，而不是額外增加一個元素。因此，同一輪循環通常不會同時出現五種核心段落。
  • 主歌—副歌形式的歌曲，也可能完全沒有橋段。

以上各點，都與AABA曲式使用橋段的方式不同。

查看英文原文

Bridge (B)

Bridge sections are a flexible section type in verse-chorus form.

  • In verse-chorus form songs, the bridge tends to appear once, followed by the last chorus (or the last prechorus and chorus) of the song.
  • Within a cycle, bridges will replace the verse and/or prechorus sections instead of being added in as an extra element. Thus, you will not usually see all five core section types in a single cycle.
  • A verse-chorus song may not have a bridge at all.

Each of these points contrasts with the way bridges are used in AABA form.

Standout Lyrics within Sections

疊句

疊句主要與AABA曲式和分節歌形式相關，但偶爾也會出現在主歌—副歌歌曲的段落內。要注意，疊句與副歌不同：疊句是段落內的歌詞，副歌則是完整、獨立的段落。

查看英文原文

Refrain

While refrains are primarily associated with AABA form and strophic form, they can occasionally be used within sections of a verse-chorus form song. However, take note that refrains are distinct from choruses—refrains are a lyric within a section, whereas a chorus is an entire standalone section.

攀升句（climb）

攀升句是具有前副歌功能的一個樂句。和疊句一樣，它只有一個樂句長，太短而無法自成段落。原文將攀升句的位置界定為歌節或主歌的最後一句。

譯註：原文以always將攀升句限定為歌節或主歌的最後一句，但下一段的例子也提到橋段末尾。兩段的範圍不完全一致；共同的判準是段落內只有一句長、具有前副歌功能的材料，不能因此把它直接升格為完整前副歌。

Dexys Midnight Runners的〈Come On Eileen〉（1982）提供一個例子：留意主歌與橋段末尾，只有一句長的攀升句，也就是唱出「Tu-ra-lu-ra……」的部分；1:13可聽到。

Climb

A climb is a phrase with prechorus function. Like the refrain, because it is only one phrase long, a climb is too short to be its own section. The climb is always the last phrase of a strophe or verse.

An example of a climb is in “Come On Eileen” by Dexys Midnight Runners (1982): listen for the one-phrase climb at the end of its verses and bridge (“Tu-ra-lu-ra…”), as heard at 1:13.

Assignments
  1. Pop Music Form—The Shape of Music Around You (.pdf). Writing assignment that asks students to find songs on their own; identify them as strophic, AABA, or verse-chorus; name the sections of the song; and justify their analyses using form vocabulary.
  2. Verse-Chorus Form (.pdf, .docx). Uses BriFormer web app to create form diagrams of pop songs in verse-chorus form. One straightforward example, and one challenging example.
  3. Reading Guide: Summach (.pdf, .docx). A series of questions to help students comprehend the most salient points of Jay Summach’s “The Structure, Function, and Genesis of the Prechorus” (2011).
