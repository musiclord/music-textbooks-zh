以藍調為基礎的圖式

Bryn Hughes

重點整理

以藍調為基礎的圖式，都包含某種變格進行。

  • 許多歌曲只反覆使用I–IV這兩個和弦，這在節奏藍調與靈魂樂中特別常見。
  • 「變格嘆息」圖式IV–iv–I，包含la–le–sol [latex](\hat{6}-\downarrow\hat{6}-\hat{5})[/latex]的聲部連接，常出現在樂句結尾。
  • 可以運用「副IV」和弦，形成雙重變格圖式：♭VII–IV–I。
  • 這些副IV和弦也能形成延伸變格進行，例如♭VI–♭III–♭VII–IV–I。

本章播放清單

以藍調為基礎的圖式，也稱「降號側」圖式，主要使用五度圈降號方向上的和聲。之所以把它們與藍調聯繫起來，是因為藍調偏好IV和弦，也就是沿五度圈往降號方向遇到的第一個和弦，而且降第七級音在藍調中非常普遍。

Key Takeaways

Blues-based schemas all include some kind of plagal motion.

  • Many songs simply use the two-chord vamp I–IV (very common in R&B and soul music).
  • The "plagal sigh" schema, IV–iv–I, includes the scale-degree voice-leading la–le–sol [latex](\hat{6}-\downarrow\hat{6}-\hat{5})[/latex] and can often be found at phrase endings.
  • "Applied" IV chords can be used to create a double-plagal schema: ♭VII–IV–I.
  • These applied IV chords can be used to create extended plagal progressions such as ♭VI–♭III–♭VII–IV–I.

Chapter Playlist

Blues-based schemas, or "flat-side" schemas, are those that mostly employ harmonies found on the "flat side" of the circle of fifths. We draw the connection to the blues here because of its propensity for using the IV chord (the first chord found in the flat-wise direction on the circle of fifths), and for the general ubiquity of the flattened seventh scale degree in blues music.

變格進行

IV在共通寫作時期的作品中，已是極常見的前屬／下屬和弦，在流行／搖滾音樂中的地位卻更突出。I與IV交替，可能源於shuffle藍調吉他伴奏中的5–6鄰音進行（例1）；許多曲風都常見這種和弦交替（例2）。

譯註：此處5–6描述伴奏中的鄰音變化，不能當成羅馬數字V–vi。原文用perhaps，將它與I/IV交替的起源聯繫只是可能解釋。

Plagal Motion

The IV chord, while certainly an extremely frequent predominant/subdominant chord in common-practice repertoire, has an even more prominent place in pop/rock music. Perhaps borne out of the 5–6 neighboring motion found in shuffle-blues guitar accompaniment patterns (Example 1), an alternation between I and IV is a common occurrence in numerous genres (Example 2).

小iv和弦

搖滾與流行音樂中，一種很常見的變格圖式，是用小iv和弦形成類似終止的手勢。它最常出現在IV–iv–I這個三和弦圖式中，通常伴隨下行旋律la–le–sol [latex](\hat{6}-\downarrow\hat{6}-\hat{5})[/latex]，例如例6的吉他聲部。半音下行，對主和弦產生特別強的趨向。

J. Kent Williams與Frank Lehman分別討論美國流行歌曲黃金年代及古典好萊塢電影配樂中的這種下行，稱之為「變格嘆息」。[1]兩位作者都認為，這種手勢會喚起懷舊與感傷。在流行音樂中，音樂人也常把這個進行，搭配帶有感傷意味的歌詞。

Green Day〈Wake Me Up When September Ends〉（例6），同時呈現上述兩個傾向：IV–iv–I進行，以及歌詞中的懷舊與感傷。

Minor iv

A very common plagal schema in rock and popular music is the use of the minor iv chord as a kind of cadential gesture. It is most commonly found as part of the three-chord schema IV–iv–I. The schema is typically accompanied by the descending melody la–le–sol [latex](\hat{6}-\downarrow\hat{6}-\hat{5})[/latex], which is found in the guitar part in Example 6. The semitone descent creates an especially strong pull to the tonic.

This descent has been referred to  by J. Kent Williams and Frank Lehman as a "plagal sigh" in Golden Era American popular song and Classic Hollywood film scores, respectively.[1] Both authors consider this gesture to invoke a sense of nostalgia and sentimentality. Indeed, even in pop music, musicians typically use this progression in conjunction with lyrics that suggest sentimentality.

"Wake Me Up When September Ends" by Green Day (Example 6) exhibits both of the tendencies discussed above: motion from IV–iv–I and a sense of nostalgia and sentimentality in the lyrics.

雙重變格

「雙重變格」是Walter Everett使用的術語：在前述變格進行中，於IV之前再加入IV/IV和弦，形成擴展。[2]更簡單的寫法是♭VII–IV–I。最有名的例子，可能就是披頭四〈Hey Jude〉的尾聲（例7）。

譯註：IV/IV表示「IV調的IV和弦」，在原調可寫成♭VII，並不是斜線低音記號。以C大調為例，是B♭–F–C。

Double-Plagal

The “double-plagal” progression (Walter Everett’s term) is an expansion of the plagal progression discussed above to include the IV/IV chord prior to the IV chord.[2] This is perhaps more simply explained as ♭VII–IV–I. The most famous instance of the double-plagal progression is likely the coda from “Hey Jude” by the Beatles (Example 7).

Example 7. The Beatles, "Hey Jude" (1968).

延伸變格

「副IV」和弦可以連續形成模進，類似共通寫作時期音樂的下行五度進行。Jimi Hendrix版本的〈Hey Joe〉（1966），主歌在E大調中，以根音下行四度的模式連續使用變格關係，形成♭VI–♭III–♭VII–IV–I。

譯註：原文說「三次」變格關係，卻列出五個和弦；這個五和弦鏈實際有四次相鄰連接。此處保留完整進行，以連續變格關係表述。E大調的和弦為C–G–D–A–E；根音可理解為依序下行四度或其轉位上行五度，方向與下行五度模進不同。

Extended Plagal

The “applied IV” chord can be used in sequence, similar to the descending-fifths progression in common-practice music. In the version of “Hey Joe” by Jimi Hendrix (1966), the verse consists of three iterations of the plagal motion in a descending-fourths pattern, which results in the progression ♭VI–♭III–♭VII–IV–I, in the key of E major.

辨認以藍調為基礎的圖式

以上各例，向前推進的感覺都來自IV到I的和聲進行。其他變化，例如IV/IV（♭VII）或小iv，都是在這個基本變格進行上的額外修飾。

Recognizing Blues-Based Schemas

In all of these examples, the sense of forward motion is created by the harmonic motion from IV to I. The other alterations, such as IV/IV (♭VII) or minor iv, are extra embellishments on this essential plagal motion.
